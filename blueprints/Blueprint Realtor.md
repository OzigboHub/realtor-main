**“Company Blueprint \+ Operating System \+ Founder War Room” as one integrated management architecture**, rather than just a Notion workspace or a collection of SOPs.

Current operating-system frameworks generally converge around the same core idea: **vision → priorities → people → processes → metrics → decisions → execution cadence**. EOS, for example, explicitly organizes around Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, and Traction, while newer founder-OS approaches emphasize context, outcomes, work, customer evidence, decisions, metrics, and weekly review. ([EOS Worldwide](https://www.eosworldwide.com/faq?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

# **2\. Company Operating System — “How does the company actually run?”**

This is where most companies are weak.

I would divide it into **10 operating systems**.

### **A. Strategy OS**

Controls:

* Vision  
* Strategic objectives  
* Annual goals  
* Quarterly priorities  
* Strategic initiatives  
* Company bets  
* Stop-doing list  
* Strategic assumptions  
* Strategic reviews

Every initiative should answer:

**Why are we doing this?**

### **B. People OS**

Controls:

* Hiring

* Training  
* Succession  
* Culture  
* Team health

The key concept is **ownership**.

Every important outcome needs:

> **One accountable owner.**

Not “the team.”

### **C. Product OS**

For a technology company, this becomes extremely important.

For every product:

# **3\. Founder War Room — “What needs my attention?”**

This is the most interesting part.

The **Founder War Room should NOT contain everything.**

It should contain only what requires founder/leadership attention.

Think of it as the company's **cockpit**.

## **Founder War Room**

### **TODAY**

**Critical Issues**

* 🔴 Critical  
* 🟠 Needs attention  
* 🟢 Normal

### **COMPANY PULSE**

| Area | Status | Key Signal |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Cash | 🟢 | Current cash position |
| Revenue | 🟢 | MTD revenue |
| Sales | 🟠 | Pipeline |
| Customers | 🟢 | Active customers |
| Product | 🟠 | Major releases |
| Engineering | 🟢 | Delivery |
| Operations | 🟠 | Blockers |
| People | 🟢 | Critical staffing |
| Legal | 🟢 | Open obligations |
| Security | 🟢 | Active incidents |

The actual indicators should be customized to the company.

# **The Founder War Room should answer 12 questions**

When you open it, you should immediately know:

1. **Where are we going?**  
2. **What matters this quarter?**  
3. **Are we winning or losing?**  
4. **What is broken?**  
5. **What requires my decision?**  
6. **What is blocked?**  
7. **Who owns each problem?**  
8. **Where is money going?**  
9. **What are customers saying?**  
10. **What risks could hurt the company?**  
11. **What decisions have recently been made?**  
12. **What should we stop doing?**

That last question is especially important.

# **The Decision Engine**

One thing I would add that many company systems underemphasize:

## **Decision Register**

Every significant decision gets recorded.

Example:

| Decision | Owner | Date | Reason | Impact | Review |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Launch Product X | CEO | Aug 14 | Market demand | High | Sept 30 |
| Hire Backend Engineer | CTO | Aug 12 | Delivery capacity | Medium | Dec |
| Pause Feature Y | Product | Aug 10 | Low adoption | Medium | Oct |

This prevents the classic:

> “Why did we decide to do this?”

six months later.

A founder OS should preserve not only **what was decided**, but **why** and when it should be reconsidered. ([100 Tasks](https://www.100tasks.com/blog/build-founder-operating-system?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

# **Then add the Company Rhythm**

The system needs a heartbeat.

A good starting structure:

### **Daily**

**15-minute execution pulse**

* What moved?  
* What's blocked?  
* What's urgent?  
* What changed?

### **Weekly**

**Leadership/Founder War Room**

Review:

* KPIs  
* Cash  
* Sales  
* Product  
* Engineering  
* Customers  
* Operations  
* People  
* Risks  
* Decisions  
* Priorities

### **Monthly**

**Business Review**

Look at:

* Financial performance  
* Product performance  
* Sales  
* Customers  
* Team  
* Operational efficiency  
* Strategic initiatives

### **Quarterly**

**Strategic Review**

Ask:

* What did we accomplish?  
* What didn't happen?  
* Why?  
* What did we learn?  
* What changed in the market?  
* What should we stop?  
* What should we start?  
* What are the next 3–5 major priorities?

EOS uses quarterly priorities (“Rocks”) and a weekly leadership meeting cadence; Scaling Up uses a broader cadence including daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual rhythms. ([EOS Worldwide](https://www.eosworldwide.com/eos-vs-scaling-up?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## **And there is one principle I'd make non-negotiable:**

**The War Room should be the front door.**

Everything else feeds it.

You shouldn't have to go hunting through 15 departments to understand the company.

The founder opens the War Room and sees:

> **Where are we → What's happening → What's broken → What matters → What decision is needed → Who owns it → What happens next.**

That is the difference between a **documentation system** and an actual **company operating system**.

If you're building this as a serious internal system, the next research step should be to turn this into a **complete Company OS specification**: architecture, modules, databases/entities, dashboards, roles/permissions, workflows, meeting cadence, KPI framework, decision system, SOP hierarchy, and the exact Founder War Room screens. That would give you something you could actually implement rather than just a business framework.
